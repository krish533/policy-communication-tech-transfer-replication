# Editor summary

## Research question

What happens around ordinary revisions of university intellectual property policies, and where along the technology transfer process do outcomes diverge when the written stance toward inventors shifts in a more supportive or more restrictive direction?

## Empirical setting

The analysis begins from the university IP policy corpus and identifies consecutive policy documents with meaningful changes in the Policy Communication Stance Index (PCSI). After mechanical screens and documentary review for comparability and timing, the preferred event study sample contains 34 revisions at 32 universities: 8 upward and 26 downward revisions.

Each revision is studied in a stacked event study using contemporaneous universities with no threshold revision in the same event window as controls. The main outcomes are invention disclosures, new patent applications, the filing margin, patents issued, and licenses and options executed.

## Main result

The clearest post revision divergence appears in licensing. The average upward minus downward licensing contrast over event years 0 through +5 is 0.495 log points (SE 0.140; nominal permutation p = 0.032). Relative to non revising controls, licensing rises after upward revisions and falls after downward revisions.

The filing margin and new patent applications move in the same direction but are estimated less precisely. Invention disclosures show no corresponding increase. The licensing result does not survive the Benjamini Hochberg adjustment across the five principal outcomes (q = 0.159), so the paper treats it as an outcome specific empirical pattern rather than evidence of a broad improvement in technology transfer.

## Robustness

The licensing estimate is nearly unchanged in the stricter post support sample and remains positive in leave one event out exercises. It is also similar when the PCSI revision threshold is raised from 0.03 to 0.04. At 0.05 the point estimate remains positive but becomes less precise as the event sample falls substantially.

## Interpretation

The paper does not argue that supportive wording itself causes more licensing. Universities choose how and when to revise their policies, and upward revisions begin from substantially lower PCSI predecessor policies than downward revisions.

The contribution is instead to make more visible an underlying governance mechanism that is difficult to observe directly: how universities structure and communicate their relationship with inventors. The concentration of the empirical pattern after disclosure is more naturally connected to post disclosure processes such as TTO routines, evaluation, continued inventor interaction, marketing, negotiation, or broader organizational change than to a simple story in which policy revisions mainly induce more faculty to disclose inventions.

The paper therefore interprets the estimates as patterns around policy revision episodes, not as structural causal effects of particular words or clauses.
